# Lunar site investigation — playable educational model

Checked 2026-11-05. NASA's LRO educator resource kit contains an activity for comparing potential south-polar base sites using temperature, access to water, solar energy, and communications. Its exercise asks learners to justify their choice with evidence; it does not identify one universally correct location. Source: NASA, *LRO Educator Resource Kit*, “Planning a Mission to the Lunar South Pole”, https://science.nasa.gov/wp-content/uploads/2024/01/lro-educator-resource-kit.pdf.

Implemented: drag a habitat token among three fictional site categories and commit a location. The selected site changes daily power and water use, and one site adds a simplified science bonus. The mission debrief identifies the choice and its trade-off. These invented numeric rules are not NASA measurements or predictions. The data is built in and works offline. Further work: add distinct temperature, possible ice and Earth-visibility layers from a reviewed NASA dataset; direct scientific observations have not yet been integrated. Do not assert that ice is guaranteed at any chosen site.

NASA also notes evidence for water both on sunlit and shadowed lunar surfaces; distribution and accessibility remain active research questions. Source: NASA Science, *Moon Water and Ices*, https://science.nasa.gov/moon/moon-water-and-ices/.
